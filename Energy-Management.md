# Energy Management

## Question 1: What activities help you relax? — Calm Quadrant

- Sleeping or taking a short nap
- Meditation and breathing exercises
- Taking a walk
- Listening to music
- Spending time on hobbies
- Talking and laughing with people I care about
- Taking a break without using screens

## Question 2: When do you find yourself in the Stress Quadrant?

- When I have too many tasks or deadlines
- When I overthink about work or studies
- When I face something difficult or unfamiliar
- When I am under pressure to perform
- When I keep working without enough rest

## Question 3: How do you understand if you are in the Excitement Quadrant?

- I feel energetic and motivated
- I am interested in what I am doing
- I can focus without feeling forced
- I feel challenged but not overwhelmed
- I enjoy the process and want to keep going

## Question 4: Sleep Is Your Superpower — Brief Paraphrase

- Sleep supports both the brain and body.
- Good sleep improves learning and memory.
- Sleep helps the brain process and store new information.
- Lack of sleep reduces attention, decision-making and emotional control.
- Poor sleep can weaken the immune system.
- Sleep affects physical health and long-term well-being.
- Regular, sufficient sleep is essential for healthy functioning.

## Question 5: What can you do to sleep better?

- Keep a consistent sleep and wake time.
- Get enough sleep every night.
- Avoid caffeine late in the day.
- Reduce bright screens before bedtime.
- Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and comfortable.
- Exercise regularly, but avoid intense exercise close to bedtime.
- Use a relaxing bedtime routine.

## Question 6: Brain-Changing Benefits of Exercise — Brief Paraphrase

- Exercise immediately improves mood.
- It can reduce stress and anxiety.
- Exercise improves attention and focus.
- Regular exercise strengthens memory.
- It supports the growth and health of brain cells.
- Long-term exercise helps protect the brain from cognitive decline.
- Exercise benefits both physical and mental health.

## Question 7: What steps can you take to exercise more?

- Start with 20–30 minutes of movement each day.
- Take short walking breaks during study or work.
- Use stairs instead of lifts when practical.
- Choose an activity I enjoy, such as walking, running or sports.
- Exercise at a fixed time in my daily routine.
- Increase intensity and duration gradually.
- Track my progress to stay consistent.
