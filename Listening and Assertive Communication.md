# Listening and Assertive Communication

## 1. What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

* Try not to get distracted by your own thoughts. Pay attention to the person who is speaking and what they are saying.
* Avoid interrupting someone while they are talking. Let them finish first and then give your response.
* Use simple phrases that encourage the other person to continue talking and show that you are interested in the conversation.
* Show that you are listening through your body language, like maintaining eye contact and nodding when appropriate.
* When the conversation is important, especially in meetings or technical discussions, take notes so you don't miss any important details.
* Paraphrase what the other person said in your own words to make sure you understood it correctly and that both of you are on the same page.

---

## 2. According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

According to Fisher's model, following are the key points of Reflective Listening:

1. **Listen carefully** – Give the speaker your full attention and avoid interrupting.
2. **Understand the message** – Focus on what the person is actually trying to communicate, not just the words they use.
3. **Reflect or repeat the main idea** – Explain the speaker's message back in your own words.
4. **Reflect the feelings** – Notice and acknowledge the emotions behind the message, such as frustration, concern, or excitement.
5. **Check your understanding** – Ask questions or confirm whether your interpretation is correct.

---

## 3. What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some obstacles I face in my listening process are:

* Getting distracted by my own thoughts or things happening around me.
* Not taking notes during important discussions can make me forget small but important details.
* Using my phone or multitasking while someone is speaking can reduce my attention.

---

## 4. What can you do to improve your listening?

* I can give my full attention to the person who is speaking and avoid distractions.
* I can stop thinking about my reply while the other person is still talking and focus on understanding them first.
* I can avoid interrupting and let the person complete what they want to say.
* I can ask questions whenever something is unclear instead of making assumptions.
* I can take notes during important meetings or technical discussions so that I don't miss any important details.

---

## 5. When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I sometimes become passive when I do not want to hurt someone's feelings or when I want to avoid an argument.

---

## 6. When do you switch to Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may become aggressive when I am angry, frustrated, or under pressure. Sometimes, I may speak loudly, interrupt others, or try to force my opinion instead of calmly discussing the situation.

---

## 7. When do you switch to Passive-Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may use passive-aggressive communication when I am upset but do not want to directly express my feelings.

---

## 8. How can you make your communication assertive?

I can make my communication more assertive by clearly expressing my thoughts, feelings, and needs while also respecting other people's opinions.
